# Trust boundary and limitations

Inputs are public caller declarations, not authenticated external observations. Do not submit secrets. A semantic agreement can still be wrong; validators may share models or correlated errors. Prompt instructions delimit data, but cannot prove immunity to prompt injection. The contract uses independent full-field agreement rather than trusting a leader-written explanation.

No external HTTP source or oracle is queried. No real-world resources are moved, reserved or guaranteed. A mathematically correct certificate proves only the accepted finite model. It does not prove that the declared model matches reality.

Input bounds protect deterministic enumeration; they also deliberately exclude larger instances. Strict semantic agreement can reject reasonable paraphrases if models interpret them differently. UNKNOWN is a business result for genuinely insufficient text, not a fallback for parser errors, provider errors or failed consensus. Such failures leave the SEALED record unchanged.

There is no administrator, upgrade path, overwrite, money custody, appeal method or automatic write retry. A record belongs to its sender address. Public read queries do not confer write access. The revision guard rejects stale writes. Ephemeral test identities, if used, have no funds and cannot be recovered after process exit; their public records remain readable.

The storage and transaction rollback behavior must also be verified on the actual GenVM runtime. Local substitute tests do not emulate node consensus, production gas metering or distributed execution.
